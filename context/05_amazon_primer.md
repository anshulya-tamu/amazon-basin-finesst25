## One river, several names
1. **Marañón + Ucayali**: Andean headwaters in Peru; they join near Nauta.
2. **Amazonas (Peru)**: past Iquitos to the Colombia–Brazil–Peru border.
3. **Solimões**: the Brazilian name from Tabatinga to the Negro confluence at Manaus. This is the study
   corridor.
4. **Amazonas (Brazil)**: below Manaus to the Atlantic near Macapá. The Madeira joins about 150 km below
   Manaus.

## Three kinds of water
- **White water** (águas brancas): turbid, café-com-leite colour from Andean sediment; nutrient-rich.
  Solimões, Madeira, Purus, Juruá, Japurá, Içá. Absorbs sunlight near the surface, but fast flow mixes the
  column.
- **Black water** (águas pretas): tea-coloured from dissolved organic carbon, acidic, little sediment.
  Rio Negro. Traps heat in a thin, warm surface layer.
- **Clear water** (águas claras): transparent and green, draining the old Brazilian and Guiana shields.
  Tapajós, Xingu, Trombetas.

## The corridor, going downstream (about 1,600 river-km)
1. **Tabatinga / Leticia**: triple border. ANA gauge 10100000. The Javari joins near Benjamin Constant.
2. **Santo Antônio do Içá**: the Içá (Putumayo) joins from the north. Main-stem gauge.
3. **Fonte Boa**: after the Jutaí joins from the south. Main-stem gauge.
4. **Juruá and Japurá**: big white-water tributaries. The Japurá links through many channels across the
   Mamirauá várzea (Mamirauá Reserve).
5. **Tefé**: Lake Tefé (ria lake) reached about 39 °C in 2023. Mamirauá Institute. INMET 82317.
6. **Coari**: Lake Coari, 642 km² in HydroLAKES. Dolphin deaths in 2023 (also at Tefé).
7. **Codajás → Purus**: the Purus joins from the south above Manacapuru. Itapéua gauge is in this stretch.
8. **Manacapuru**: last main-stem gauge before Manaus (14100000, verify). Mean Q 102,458 m³/s (1973–2012).
9. **Manaus**: the Negro meets the Solimões (Meeting of the Waters). INMET 82331.

## The hydrological year
- **Rain, central Amazon:** wet Dec–May, dry Jun–Nov. Manaus: 2,323 mm/yr; driest month August, 68 mm.
- **Solimões at Tabatinga:** rising Jan–Mar, high Apr–Jun (peak May), falling Jul–Aug, low Sep–Oct.
- **Water level at Manaus:**
  - enchente (rising): Dec–May
  - cheia (high, peak ~June)
  - vazante (falling): Aug–Sep
  - seca (low): Oct–Nov

## 2023 and 2024 droughts
- **Cause:** El Niño plus an unusually warm tropical North Atlantic.
- **Air temperature:** anomalies of +1.8, +2.2 and +2.7 °C in Aug, Sep and Oct 2023.
- **Manaus:** lowest Rio Negro level on record in October 2023.
- **Tabatinga:** low but not a record (see 04).
- **Lakes:** shallow lakes passed 37 °C (up to 41 °C); dolphin and fish deaths. The main stem stayed at
  30–32 °C.
- **2024:** the drought returned and broke low-water records again at several gauges.

## Who measures what
- **ANA:** water agency; HidroWeb data.
- **SGB (formerly CPRM):** operates much of the gauge network.
- **INMET:** weather stations.
- **INPA, Manaus:** Val lab; aquatic biology, field temperatures.
- **Mamirauá Institute, Tefé:** lakes and várzea; Ayan Fleischmann.
- **SO-HYBAM:** discharge, sediment and chemistry observatory.
- **IPH-UFRGS, Porto Alegre:** MGB-IPH model.
